LoopTodo Privacy Policy

Effective November 12, 2026. Applies to the LoopTodo Android app (com.parker1978.looptodo).

TL;DR

To put it simply:

  • I don't collect your data, and I never see it.
  • Your lists, todos and settings stay on your device unless you choose to share them yourself, for example with Export or AI assist.
  • From version 3.0.0, LoopTodo uses the internet for one thing: a todo's location. Looking a place up sends what you typed to Google Maps, with roughly where you are if you allow it, and a todo with a location shows a Google map. A todo with no location sends nothing.
  • From version 3.4.0, a todo or a list can remind you when you arrive at a place, when you leave it, or when to set off to get there on time, and you can keep places you use often (Home, Work, favourites). Only if you set one up and allow it, LoopTodo then uses your location, precise or approximate as you choose, also in the background. Arriving and leaving are checked on your phone by Google Play services, and LoopTodo sends nothing for them. For a "leave in time" reminder, where you're going and where you start from (where you are, or a place you chose) are sent to Google to work out how long the trip takes. One that starts from a place you chose doesn't use your location at all.
  • To check for updates, LoopTodo asks the Google Play Store app on your phone, and Google Play does the checking. None of your lists or settings go with it.

Overview

LoopTodo respects your privacy. Its developer does not collect, store or receive any of your data. Your lists, todos, tags, loop history and settings are kept in the app's private storage on your device. They leave it only if you choose to share them yourself, for example with Export or AI assist.

The one thing LoopTodo sends anywhere itself is what's needed for a todo's location: the place you search for, roughly where you are if you allow it, the place whose map is shown, and, for a "leave in time" reminder, where you start from (where you are, or a place you chose) and the place you're going. That goes to Google Maps and nowhere else, and is explained under Locations (Google Maps) and Location reminders below.

Information Collection and Use

LoopTodo does not:

  • Collect personal information
  • Use analytics, crash reporting or tracking tools
  • Send your lists, todos, tags, loop history or settings to any server
  • Keep a history of where you've been, or follow where you go
  • Use your location in the background, unless you set up a location reminder and allow it (see Location reminders)
  • Require an account or sign-in
  • Display ads or use advertising SDKs

Everything you enter stays on your device unless you choose to share it yourself, for example with Export or AI assist, or look a location up as described below. The app's developer never sees any of it.

Locations (Google Maps)

From version 3.0.0, a todo can have a location, and LoopTodo uses Google Maps to look places up and to show a map. This is the only thing LoopTodo uses the internet for, and it's optional: a todo with no location sends nothing.

Looking a place up. When you type a place or an address under Location and tap to look it up, LoopTodo sends what you typed to Google Maps (the Google Maps Platform Places service) to get matching places. It is sent only when you ask for the lookup, never as you type.

Your approximate location. The first time you look a place up, Android asks whether LoopTodo may see your approximate location. If you allow it, LoopTodo reads roughly where the phone is at that moment and sends it to Google Maps with what you typed, so the places near you are found, and uses it on your phone to say how far each one is and list the nearest first. For looking places up, LoopTodo reads it only while a place is being looked up, and doesn't keep it. If you say no, places are still looked up, just not in order of distance, and you can change your answer at any time in Android's settings. Location reminders, below, are the only thing that uses precise location or location in the background.

The map. A todo with a looked-up location shows a small Google map of the streets around it. To draw it, LoopTodo asks Google for the map around that place each time the map is shown. Which place it is goes to Google; nothing else about the todo does.

Keeping a place up to date. For a place picked from Google's search, LoopTodo keeps the name and address you picked, Google's ID for the place and its position on the map, all on your device. About every 25 days it asks Google for the place's position again, using only that ID. If it can't for 30 days, it forgets the position until it can.

Saved places. From version 3.4.0 you can keep places you use often: Home, Work and favourites you name yourself. They're kept on your device, with the same details as a todo's place, are included in your backups, and are kept up to date the same way. A reminder can use a saved place, or another place of its own, instead of its todo's place, and a leave by reminder can start from one.

What Google receives. As with any request over the internet, Google also receives your device's IP address and some technical details about the device and the app. Each request carries a key that identifies the LoopTodo app, not you: LoopTodo has no accounts, and gives Google no name or email address. Google handles this information under the Google Maps Platform terms and Google's Privacy Policy. The developer receives none of it, and sees only how many requests the app makes in total, never what was searched for or by whom.

Without Google Maps. If Google's search can't be reached, LoopTodo asks your phone's own address lookup instead (part of Android, provided by Google on most phones), which is sent the same text and, if you allowed it, the area around you. You can also keep a location exactly as you typed it without looking it up, which sends nothing.

Navigating. Tapping a todo's location lets you pick a maps app on your phone, such as Google Maps or Waze. LoopTodo hands that app the place's address or position so it can take you there. What that app does with it is covered by its own privacy policy.

Location reminders

From version 3.4.0, a todo or a list can have a reminder that goes off when you arrive at a place, when you leave it, or in time to arrive by a time you choose ("leave by"). They're optional: you set each one up yourself, in the todo's or list's reminder sheet, and nothing described here happens without one.

What you're asked to allow. Before Android asks, LoopTodo explains what location reminders need. Android then lets you choose precise or approximate location, and LoopTodo goes with your choice. Then it asks for location all the time (in the background), because they have to work while LoopTodo is closed. Precise is needed for arrive and leave reminders. With approximate, only leave by reminders work, from roughly where you are (Android gives an app with approximate location your position to within a few kilometres). A leave by reminder that starts from a place you chose (Home, say) instead of where you are needs no location at all, and nothing is asked. You can refuse, or change your answers at any time in Android's settings. Without location all the time, arrive and leave reminders are paused, and a leave by reminder from where you are goes off from its last estimate of the trip.

Arrive and leave reminders. LoopTodo asks Google Play services, which is part of Android on most phones, to watch an area around the place of each of these reminders (a "geofence"): its todo's place, a saved place, or a place of the reminder's own, and to tell LoopTodo when you arrive in it or leave it. Google Play services does this on your phone, using the phone's location as it does for other apps. LoopTodo itself doesn't receive or keep your position for this: it's only told that you arrived at or left one of those places. It keeps which of them you're at right now, on your phone, for the Here list, until you leave (after 12 hours without word from Google Play services it stops counting it). LoopTodo sends nothing anywhere for these reminders. A place stops being watched once no todo or list with a reminder there has anything left to do.

Leave by reminders. To work out when you need to set off, LoopTodo checks the trip a few times before you go: about three hours before the time you want to arrive, and 30 and 10 minutes before you'd need to leave. Each time, it sends where the trip starts and the reminder's place (Google's ID for it, or its position on the map) to Google's Routes service, with how you're travelling and, for public transport, when you want to arrive. The trip starts where the phone is, which it reads for that check, or, when you chose a place to start from (a saved place, or one of the reminder's own), at that place's position on the map, and then where the phone is isn't read. Google answers with how long the trip takes, with live traffic when you're driving. While you set one up, the reminder sheet asks once more, to show you when you'd leave. If, at one of those checks, a trip from where you are finds the phone already at the place (with precise location), nothing is sent and the reminder doesn't go off. LoopTodo keeps the last travel time, and where the phone was when it was worked out, on your phone, so it doesn't ask again when you haven't moved. Each check replaces them, and they aren't in backups.

What Google receives for leave by reminders is the same kind of request as for looking places up, described under Locations (Google Maps): the positions above, your device's IP address and a key that identifies the app, not you. Google handles it under the Google Maps Platform terms and Google's Privacy Policy. The developer receives none of it.

Notifications and navigating. The countdown to when to leave is a notification on your phone (a Live Update on Android 16 and later), and sends nothing. Its Navigate button opens Google Maps, or another maps app, with the place, as tapping a todo's location does.

Update Checks (Google Play)

From version 2.3.0, LoopTodo uses Google Play's in-app updates library to tell you when a newer version is out. When LoopTodo opens, and when you tap Settings → Check for update, it asks the Google Play Store app on your phone whether there's an update. LoopTodo doesn't contact Google for this itself: the Play Store app checks with Google, as it does for its own updates.

According to Google, this check sends Google information about your device and the version of LoopTodo you have, so Google Play can tell whether an update exists and how big it is. Google says this information is encrypted, isn't shared with third parties, and is deleted after a set time. It's handled by Google under the Google Play Terms of Service and Google's Privacy Policy. None of your lists, todos or settings are sent, and the developer receives nothing from it.

If you tap Install, Google Play downloads and installs the update. What's new, on Google Play opens LoopTodo's page in the Play Store app. The What's new notes LoopTodo shows after an update are built into the app and need no connection. Only copies of LoopTodo installed from Google Play check for updates this way.

Backups

LoopTodo is excluded from Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer. If you choose Settings → Export, the app writes a backup file to a location you pick with the Android file picker. Where that file goes afterwards (for example a cloud drive you choose) is up to you and covered by that service's privacy policy. Import reads a file you choose and replaces the app's data with its contents.

AI Assist (Optional)

From a list's ⋮ menu, LoopTodo can prepare a copy of that list as plain text for you to give to an AI app of your choice. You can use it to suggest tags, sections, an order, priorities, steps or tidier wording, to comment on your loop history, or to draft a new list. LoopTodo doesn't connect to any AI service, and the developer never sees what you send. The text leaves your phone only if you send it yourself with Share, Copy or Save file. Whatever you send is then handled under that app's or service's own privacy policy and terms.

The text holds only what the task needs: the list's name, its todos and dividers, and, depending on the task, their tags, priorities, the todos you pick, or the list's loop history. The Add tags and Draft a new list tasks also include the names of all the tags you use in LoopTodo, including tags from other lists, so the AI can reuse them. Descriptions are included only if you tick Descriptions, and completed todos are included unless you untick them. Todos from other lists, due dates, reminders and anything else on your phone are never included. Show what's sent shows exactly what will be sent.

When you bring an answer back with Paste answer or Open file, LoopTodo reads it on your phone and shows you every change before anything is saved. Nothing changes until you tap Apply, and Undo reverses the changes. LoopTodo reads the clipboard only when you tap Paste answer, and reads only the file you pick with Open file.

Permissions

  • Internet and network state: from version 3.0.0, to look up a todo's location and show its map with Google Maps, as explained above. Nothing else uses them.
  • Approximate location: from version 3.0.0, and only if you allow it, to find places near you when you look a location up and list the nearest first.
  • Precise location and location all the time (in the background): from version 3.4.0, and only if you set up a location reminder and allow them, for arrive, leave and leave by reminders, as explained under Location reminders. You can choose approximate instead, for leave by reminders only.
  • Live Updates: from version 3.4.0, so the countdown to when to leave can show as a Live Update on Android 16 and later.
  • Notifications: to show reminders you set on todos.
  • Alarms & reminders: so reminders fire on time.
  • Run at startup: to restore reminders and scheduled list loops after your phone restarts.
  • Full-screen alerts: for reminders you set to ring like an alarm, so they can show an alarm screen over the lock screen. On Android 14 and later you allow this yourself; without it, they ring as a pop-up notification.
  • Display over other apps (called Appear on top on some phones): only if you turn it on, so those alarm screens can also open while you're using another app. LoopTodo shows nothing else over other apps.
  • Prevent phone from sleeping: briefly, so background work such as a scheduled list loop can finish.

Apart from location, which is yours to allow or refuse, none of these give LoopTodo access to your personal information. To list the maps apps you can navigate with, LoopTodo checks whether Google Maps, Waze or other maps apps are installed; that stays on your phone. AI assist needs no extra permissions: Share, Copy, Save file, Paste answer and Open file use Android's standard share sheet, clipboard and file picker.

Data Retention and Deletion

The developer collects no data, so nothing is retained by the developer. What Google receives for update checks, for looking up locations and showing maps, and for working out trips for leave by reminders, is kept by Google as described above and under Google's Privacy Policy. Delete todos or lists in the app, or uninstall LoopTodo (or clear its storage in Android settings), to remove all of its data. Copies you've sent elsewhere yourself, with Export or AI assist, are kept by wherever you sent them, and deleting data in LoopTodo doesn't remove them.

Age Appropriateness

LoopTodo contains no objectionable content and is suitable for users of all ages. There are no in-app purchases, advertisements, or social features.
